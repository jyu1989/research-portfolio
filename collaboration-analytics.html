PRODUCT ANALYTICS · BEHAVIORAL DATA · ANONYMIZED INDUSTRY PARTNERSHIP

Turning collaboration data into product insights.

In an applied research partnership with an anonymized workplace collaboration software company, we used digital collaboration activity to construct organizational networks and study employee turnover. The partner company integrated the research-informed analytics dashboard into its application.

01 / THE OPPORTUNITY

Workplace collaboration already leaves a digital trace.

Employee surveys capture perceptions, but teams also generate behavioral data as they communicate and collaborate on tasks. the company's platform created an opportunity to examine whether patterns of interaction revealed useful information about organizational connections and later turnover.

The question was whether raw collaboration records could become interpretable network insights, instead of remaining disconnected activity logs.

02 / ANALYTICAL WORK

Turning activity records into organizational networks.

I engineered network features from collaboration traces of approximately 2,400 employees across more than 20 organizations, examining how individual and organizational network structures related to subsequent turnover during a six-month window. The analysis compared statistical and predictive approaches and translated abstract network measures into interpretable workforce insights.

04 / MY CONTRIBUTION

From data engineering to product-relevant findings.

I transformed collaboration data into analyzable network structures, engineered network measures, contributed to statistical and predictive model evaluation, and translated findings for the applied partnership. The company subsequently integrated the research-informed dashboard into its product.

Methods, evaluation and limitations

Network construction included task-collaboration and communication relationships. Analyses considered individual and organization-level network features, multilevel logistic regression, Random Forest, and Bayesian Rule Sets, with train/test evaluation, cross-validation, and ROC-based assessment.

We examined associations between collaboration-network features and subsequent turnover using observational data, comparing multilevel logistic regression, Random Forest, and Bayesian Rule Sets. These findings describe relationships in the data rather than causal effects.
