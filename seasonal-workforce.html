ANONYMIZED CLIENT CASE · PEOPLE ANALYTICS · WORKFORCE MEASUREMENT

Finding early turnover signals in a seasonal workforce.

A consumer retail contact center faced repeated employee exits during the weeks following onboarding. I led an applied research partnership to identify signals of turnover risk earlier and to assess whether automated and human performance ratings were telling the same story.

01 / The operational problem

Hiring was broad. Attrition did much of the filtering.

With few barriers to entry for temporary employees, the contact center recruited broadly and invested in onboarding and training. Employees then exited in waves over the following one to two months, leaving a much smaller group available for the peak-demand period.

Central question: What early indicators of employee turnover can we identify and capture?

The business relevance was practical: could signals available in the first weeks help inform recruitment, onboarding, and training decisions before further resources were committed?

02 / Research leadership

From operational records to actionable questions.

I led the applied research partnership and guided four student analytics teams working with longitudinal operational records. The wider dataset contained 12,656 weekly observations from 105 full-time and 2,290 temporary employees. The teams used analysis-specific subsets, so those totals should not be confused with model sample sizes.

Workstream A · Early turnover risk

Examined whether training measures, Week 1 to 2 performance evaluations, personal time, infractions, and coaching behavior were associated with later employment status.

Workstream B · Measurement validity

Compared AI-generated and manual employee ratings, their distributions, and their associations with operational KPIs such as sales conversion.

03 / Finding A

Early behavior contained signals of later retention.

One analysis found that Week 2 personal-time percentage and recorded infractions were negatively associated with staying through the season. Another analysis identified differences among termination categories associated with training scores, early AI evaluations, conversion measures, and infractions.

Decision implication: Early indicators could serve as flags for further evaluation, not automatic reasons to reject or terminate employees. The analyses establish associations, not a validated pre-hire screening instrument.

04 / Finding B

AI and human evaluations did not tell the same story.

The client also used automated and manual employee ratings. We examined whether they agreed, and whether their relationships with operational KPIs differed.

REPORTED AGREEMENT
~0.30

Approximate correlation between automated and manual scores: weak agreement, not evidence that either system was inherently correct.

DIFFERENT DISTRIBUTIONS

Different scoring patterns

Manual scores clustered at common values (such as 0, 0.5, and 0.75), while automated scores were more continuously distributed.

Automated scores were more strongly associated with several reported performance KPIs in these analyses. But KPI correlations alone cannot determine which scores are more valid: sales results may reflect factors outside an employee's control, and the data contained missingness and limited explanatory power.

Measurement lesson: before automating workforce decisions, establish what each rating measures, and whether it is fit for the decision.
05 / Business impact

Evidence that informed a real change in hiring practices.

BUSINESS IMPACT

Research informed a change in recruitment practices.

Adopted: The client shifted from broad recruitment toward more selective hiring informed by the project's early-turnover findings.

Reported benefit: The partner reported improved hiring and training efficiency.

Evidence boundary: We do not have public quantitative estimates of the improvement or a causal evaluation linking the change to efficiency gains.

RESEARCH EVIDENCE

Early behaviors were associated with later retention; ratings differed in important ways.

REPORTED RESPONSE

The client moved toward more selective recruitment informed by project findings.

The client subsequently adopted a more selective recruitment approach informed by the early-turnover findings. According to the partner, this change improved the efficiency of hiring and training. Exact efficiency metrics were not provided, and the analyses did not establish that the recruitment changes alone caused the improvement.

The collaboration also continued into the design of repeated employee surveys intended to investigate onboarding, culture transmission, and employee experiences alongside operational outcomes.

06 / What I owned

Research leadership, analytical judgment, and stakeholder translation.

My contribution

Framed applied research questions with the industry partner, guided four analytics teams, assessed methodological choices and limitations, synthesized findings, and scoped follow-on research with stakeholders.

Interpretation boundaries

Observational associations are not causal effects. Analyses used different samples and outcomes. Missing data and weak fits in some models limited prediction claims; the AI to human agreement result does not establish which system is more accurate.

Methods and evidence details Expand details

Methods used across teams included multinomial and binary logistic regression, correlation, group comparisons, and linear regression. Training, Week 1 to 2 measures and subsequent employment statuses were examined with different specifications.

For one retention analysis, Week 2 personal time (reported p = .0195) and infractions (reported p = .0038) were negatively associated with full-season retention. Another team's multinomial analysis identified varying associations across turnover categories. The reported automated to manual correlation was approximately 0.3.

Supporting materials: four 2025 course research presentations. Findings were synthesized from separate student teams, not a single integrated validated prediction model. Public text and illustrations are generalized; no raw client records or exact confidential workforce distributions are reproduced.
