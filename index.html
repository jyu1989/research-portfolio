ABOUT

Researcher first. Methodologist when the problem requires it.

My work sits at the intersection of organizational behavior, people analytics, measurement science, and applied research. I am most interested in questions where the answer depends on how people are connected, how experiences unfold over time, or how measurement choices shape the conclusions we draw.

I currently serve as an Assistant Professor of Management at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln and have worked across academic research, industry consulting, early-stage product development, and applied people analytics.

WHAT I'M EXPLORING

Human judgment and collaboration with AI

My recent interests build on my research in human behavior, teams, and measurement. I'm exploring how people decide whether AI-generated work is good enough to trust, and what happens when people and AI agents work together.

How people judge AI-generated work

What makes an output technically sound but still unacceptable to its audience? I'm interested in how people form quality standards, and how perceived authenticity, trust, and user preferences shape acceptance.

How AI agents collaborate

When do multiple agents improve a result, and when do they repeat or reinforce one another's mistakes? I'm interested in complementary roles, critical review, and how human oversight shapes team performance.

I'm exploring these questions hands-on through my multi-agent research workflow and AI-assisted app prototypes. These are emerging research interests and experiments, not claims of completed AI evaluation studies.
