01 · WORKOUTS ON THE GO

On the Spot

What if I could start exercising before the urge disappears?

The need I noticed

Sometimes I feel like exercising while lying in bed. But once I think about getting ready and going to the gym, the urge fades. The problem isn't always wanting to work out. It's the effort between wanting to and actually starting.

My product response

I built a workout coach that starts with the person's immediate circumstances: where they are, how much time they have, how they feel, and what equipment is nearby. The interface also includes strength logs and daily movement goals.

What I'd test next

Does a context-aware suggestion make people more likely to begin a workout when they feel motivated, compared with having to choose or plan one themselves?

Product decision: Remove the need for a gym trip or a fixed plan as prerequisites for getting started.

Hush prototype · Built with Lovable

02 · SUPPORT FOR NEW PARENTS

Hush

Why is my baby crying, and what should I try?

What I heard from parents

As a mother, I know how difficult it can be to tell what a crying baby needs. In informal conversations, other new parents expressed a similar wish: they wanted to understand why their babies were crying.

My product response

I used Lovable to prototype an experience where parents could share a photo or short video, explore possible explanations and age-appropriate soothing ideas, and give feedback about what helped. The feedback loop is a concept for future personalization, not an established capability.

What I'd test next

Do parents find the suggestions useful, and can they distinguish a possible explanation from a reliable assessment? I'd also want to evaluate accuracy and safety before anyone relied on the guidance.

Product decision: Support parents in deciding what to try next without presenting an AI-generated interpretation as a diagnosis.

WHAT THESE PROJECTS SHOW

I can investigate a need and build something to explore it.

My research training helps me notice behavioral problems and ask better questions. AI-assisted development gives me a way to turn those questions into interfaces I can put in front of people. The next step is to test whether the solutions actually help.

These are personal, exploratory prototypes. The conversations were informal, and I have not conducted formal usability studies or validated either app's effectiveness. Hush's proposed interpretations and personalization have not been clinically or technically validated.
