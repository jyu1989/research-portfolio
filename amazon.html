THE CHALLENGE

Which creative attributes actually add information?

The team wanted to understand advertising liking, purchase intent, and brand perceptions. Overlapping predictors and repeated ratings meant that simple significance tests could be misleading.

01 / MEASUREMENT

Start with construct quality

Reviewed survey measures, factor structure, reliability, and response-quality safeguards.

Decision guidance

Validate what a score represents before interpreting relationships.

02 / ANALYSIS

Separate overlapping predictors

Used regression, random-effects meta-analysis, and multilevel modeling to examine associations across creatives.

Decision guidance

Compare bivariate and adjusted effects, including clustering and heterogeneity.

03 / INTERPRETATION

Move beyond p-values

Explained differences between statistical significance, effect magnitude, and consistency.

Decision guidance

Prioritize practically informative effects and communicate uncertainty.

Research impact

Helped reframe interpretation away from lists of significant coefficients and toward the magnitude, independence, robustness, and practical relevance of effects. Returned for follow-up consultation on measurement and scale development.

Technical methods and research limitations Expand details

Analyses: Creative-level regression, random-effects meta-analysis, and multilevel models for repeated or clustered ratings; measurement and factor-structure checks.

Methodological judgment: Considered multicollinearity, response quality, higher-level sample size, scale development, effect-size interpretation, and the information loss caused by dichotomizing continuous responses.

Limits: Observational survey associations do not establish causal effects. This page does not claim measured business lift, product launches, or adoption of specific recommendations. Findings are generalized to protect client confidentiality.
