01 / BUSINESS QUESTION

Which advertising attributes provide meaningful information?

Across 134 creative-level samples, the research team examined how advertising attributes related to ad liking, purchase intent, and brand perceptions. The goal was to interpret those relationships in a way that could inform future research and measurement decisions.

ANALYTICAL QUESTIONIs the relationship statistically significant?
→
DECISION-READY QUESTIONHow large, consistent, and interpretable is the relationship?
02 / ANALYTICAL CHALLENGE

Significance alone was not enough.

Advertising attributes can overlap, and the relationships observed across creatives may vary. Interpreting isolated correlations or p-values without considering effect size, measurement quality, clustering, and heterogeneity can leave stakeholders with an incomplete picture.

03 / MY APPROACH

Compare the evidence, then explain what it means.

I built analyses and visualizations in R, considered regression, random-effects meta-analysis, and multilevel modeling, and advised on the interpretation of effects across creative samples. The consulting work also included questionnaire and scale measurement considerations.

MEASUREMENT

Check the constructs

Reviewed survey measures, factor structure, reliability, and response quality.

MODEL COMPARISON

Consider alternative specifications

Examined overlapping predictors, clustered ratings, and heterogeneity across creative samples.

INTERPRETATION

Prioritize meaningful effects

Distinguished statistical significance from effect magnitude, consistency, and practical relevance.

04 / CONSULTING OUTCOME

Decision-ready research guidance, followed by further consultation.

I delivered analytical results, visualizations, and methodological recommendations that helped the team evaluate advertising evidence beyond lists of significant coefficients. The engagement led to follow-up consultation on measurement and scale development.

Technical methods and research considerations Expand details

Analyses: Creative-level regression, random-effects meta-analysis, and multilevel models for repeated or clustered ratings; measurement and factor-structure checks.

Methodological judgment: Considered multicollinearity, response quality, higher-level sample size, scale development, effect-size interpretation, and information loss from dichotomizing continuous responses.

Interpretation: Survey associations are observational and do not establish causal advertising effects. The documented outcome is consulting analysis and methodological guidance rather than a measured campaign lift.
