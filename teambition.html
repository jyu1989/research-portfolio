DIGITAL BEHAVIOR · ORGANIZATIONAL NETWORKS

Teambition Digital Collaboration & Turnover

An industry research partnership using passive digital collaboration data to model how employees were connected through work and communication — and whether those patterns were associated with later turnover.

Context

Most workforce research relies on surveys. Teambition provided an opportunity to use digital collaboration traces generated through everyday work activity and examine whether network position contained useful information about later outcomes.

My role

  • Helped transform collaboration activity into analyzable task and communication networks.
  • Engineered individual- and organization-level network measures in R.
  • Contributed to model comparison and interpretation.
  • Connected behavioral network features to subsequent turnover outcomes.

Approach

  • Constructed task-collaboration and communication networks.
  • Calculated social-network features at individual and organizational levels.
  • Compared multilevel logistic regression, Random Forest, and Bayesian Rule Sets.
  • Used train/test evaluation, cross-validation, and ROC-based model assessment.

What we learned

Turnover was related not only to individual network position but also to broader organizational network structure. The project illustrated how passive behavioral data can add a structural view of employee experience that surveys alone may miss.

Important limitation

The predictive models were exploratory research models rather than a deployed production system. I therefore describe this as workforce analytics and model evaluation, not a live attrition product.

Why this project matters: it demonstrates experience with behavioral telemetry, organizational network analysis, feature engineering, and predictive modeling on real enterprise data.
