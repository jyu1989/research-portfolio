Turning collaboration data into product insights.

In an applied research partnership with Teambition, we used digital collaboration activity to construct organizational networks and study employee turnover. The research-informed dashboard was subsequently integrated into the company's application.

01 / THE OPPORTUNITY

Workplace collaboration already leaves a digital trace.

Employee surveys capture perceptions, but teams also generate behavioral data as they communicate and collaborate on tasks. Teambition's platform created an opportunity to examine whether patterns of interaction revealed useful information about organizational connections and later turnover.

The question was whether raw collaboration records could become interpretable network insights, instead of remaining disconnected activity logs.

02 / ANALYTICAL WORK

Turning activity records into organizational networks.

I engineered network features from collaboration traces of 2,413 employees across 21 companies, examining how individual and organizational network structures related to subsequent turnover during a six-month window. The analysis compared statistical and predictive approaches and translated abstract network measures into interpretable workforce insights.

03 / PRODUCT OUTCOME

Research findings were incorporated into the product.

Adopted by Teambition

Research-informed organizational network insights were translated into an analytics dashboard that Teambition integrated into its application. The collaboration bridged research analysis and a customer-facing product feature.

That is the concrete product outcome. The turnover models themselves were exploratory research analyses, not a deployed live attrition prediction system. We do not claim that the dashboard improved employee retention or that an individual model reached production.

04 / MY CONTRIBUTION

From data engineering to product-relevant findings.

I transformed collaboration data into analyzable network structures, engineered network measures, contributed to statistical and predictive model evaluation, and translated findings for the applied partnership. The company subsequently integrated the research-informed dashboard into its product.

Methods, evaluation and limitations

Network construction included task-collaboration and communication relationships. Analyses considered individual and organization-level network features, multilevel logistic regression, Random Forest, and Bayesian Rule Sets, with train/test evaluation, cross-validation, and ROC-based assessment.

The project was exploratory and observational. Network associations with turnover should not be interpreted as causal effects. The available evidence does not establish production deployment of the prediction models, dashboard usage rates, or measured business outcomes after integration.
