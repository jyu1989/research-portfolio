THE CHALLENGE

What could existing workforce data tell us—and what couldn't it?

The initial engagement explored seasonal employee performance and turnover. The key contribution was moving beyond descriptive correlations toward questions stakeholders could investigate and act on.

01 / MEASUREMENT

Human vs. AI evaluations

Finding: The two evaluation approaches showed low agreement.

Recommendation

Assess alignment and validity before treating scores as interchangeable.

02 / RETENTION

Signals in the first weeks

Finding: Early behavioral measures predicted later workforce outcomes.

Recommendation

Investigate the mechanisms before translating risk signals into employee interventions.

03 / COACHING

More wasn't necessarily better

Finding: Coaching analyses did not demonstrate the anticipated benefits.

Recommendation

Examine coaching quality, context, and employee experiences—not quantity alone.

My role

  • Framed the business questions and guided four student analysis teams.
  • Reviewed analytical approaches, evidence, and limitations.
  • Translated findings into stakeholder recommendations.
  • Scoped the next phase with HR and operational partners.

What happened next

The organization agreed to continue the collaboration. The next phase is designed to examine onboarding, work stress, culture transmission, and reasons for leaving through repeated surveys connected to workforce records.

Design and stakeholder alignment are in progress; these are not completed intervention results.

Methods, research design, and limitations Expand details

Phase 1: Retrospective analysis of operational workforce records across retention, coaching, evaluation, and work activity. Techniques included employee-group comparisons, regression, and examination of human–AI rating agreement.

Phase 2: Prospective longitudinal employee listening, with survey timing, constructs, participant burden, and linkage to operational data under development.

Interpretation: Operational associations alone do not establish why employees leave or whether changing coaching causes better outcomes. No proprietary metrics, raw records, or internal visualizations are disclosed.
