Building a Multi-Agent Research Team

Using multiple AI models as research assistants and critical reviewers while retaining human responsibility for theory, evidence, and simulation validity.

← Back to selected work

The research problem

Developing an agent-based simulation requires translating abstract organizational theory into explicit behavioral rules and evaluating whether those rules represent the intended mechanisms. AI-assisted coding can accelerate development, but plausible-looking code and reasoning may still be conceptually incorrect.

My role and tools

I configured multiple AI agents in Cursor, drawing on models including ChatGPT, Claude, and Muse. I supplied domain-specific knowledge, guided the research questions, assigned assistant and critical-review roles, and retained responsibility for theoretical judgments.

Workflow

  1. Specify theoretical assumptions and simulation objectives.
  2. Ask AI assistants to propose or refine approaches and code.
  3. Use other models as independent reviewers to identify gaps or inconsistencies.
  4. Check disputed outputs against underlying evidence and domain knowledge.
  5. Iterate on the simulation with human judgment throughout.

AI reliability lesson

AI models sometimes produce persuasive but inaccurate claims. I use cross-model challenges to surface possible hallucinations, then independently examine evidence. Multiple models agreeing does not by itself establish correctness, especially when they may share the same assumptions or errors.

Current status and what this demonstrates

This case documents an AI-assisted research workflow and simulation-development experience, not a validated autonomous research system or a controlled evaluation of multi-agent accuracy. It demonstrates hands-on experimentation, conceptual oversight, and attention to trustworthy AI-assisted research.
